# Common Misconceptions — Module 04

**Module:** Target-State Architecture and Transformation Roadmaps  
**Audience:** Instructors (use to preempt and correct)  
**Case study:** NorthStar Financial Services (fictional)

---

## Misconception 1 — “Target state means greenfield”

**Reality:** Target state is a coherent future operating landscape—often pattern-based—with retain and coexistence as first-class design choices.

**Correction move:** Ask what happens to StarCore and regulatory evidence in year one.

## Misconception 2 — “Migrate to cloud is a strategy”

**Reality:** Cloud is a destination/platform choice. Disposition still requires rehost/replatform/refactor/replace/retire/retain/consolidate.

**Correction move:** Ban the word “migrate” in disposition cells unless paired with a strategy.

## Misconception 3 — “TIME and 7R are the same”

**Reality:** TIME is portfolio posture; 7R-style strategies are execution paths. Tolerate≈Retain often, but not always.

**Correction move:** Map one TIME=Migrate item to two different strategies and compare.

## Misconception 4 — “Transition architecture = program milestones”

**Reality:** Transitions specify interim systems of record, interfaces, controls, and exit criteria.

**Correction move:** Require students to name who writes customer data in Transition B.

## Misconception 5 — “Temporary integrations are fine without end dates”

**Reality:** Undated bridges become the architecture.

**Correction move:** Add a temporary interfaces register with end dates linked to exit criteria.

## Misconception 6 — “Roadmaps are project plans”

**Reality:** Architecture roadmaps communicate landscape sequencing, value, and dependencies; delivery plans own task detail.

**Correction move:** Strip sprint tasks from submitted roadmaps; keep waves and outcomes.

## Misconception 7 — “More detail = better target”

**Reality:** Over-specified inventories freeze politics and rot quickly. Patterns + dispositions scale better.

**Correction move:** Cap application detail; demand interface and ownership clarity.

## Misconception 8 — “Value and risk sequencing are opposites”

**Reality:** Best early items often score high on both (e.g., partner API platform, guardrails).

**Correction move:** Use the value-vs-risk board; kill low/low items explicitly.

## Misconception 9 — “Architecture can avoid picking winners”

**Reality:** Consolidate requires survivors. Avoidance retains cost.

**Correction move:** Force a CRM survivor ADR decision in demo.

## Misconception 10 — “Security comes later in Module 07”

**Reality:** Module 07 deepens controls, but coexistence without identity/audit design is negligent even in Module 04.

**Correction move:** Grade lightly but mark missing dual-write controls as a gap.
