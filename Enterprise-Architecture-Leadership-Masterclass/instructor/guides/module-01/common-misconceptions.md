# Common Misconceptions — Module 01: The Enterprise Architect’s Role

**Audience:** Instructors  
**Case study:** NorthStar Financial Services (fictional)

Use these as rapid corrections. Quote the misconception, then the reframe.

---

## Misconception 1 — “EA is just a more senior solution architect.”

**Reframe:** Scope and horizon differ. SA optimizes a solution boundary; EA optimizes cross-domain decision quality, reuse, and enterprise risk visibility. Seniority is not the definition.

## Misconception 2 — “We need an ARB for every project to have governance.”

**Reframe:** Broad ARBs create queues and bypasses. Governance = principles + guardrails + narrow gates for material decisions. NorthStar Year 1 should bias to guardrails.

## Misconception 3 — “Federated architecture means no enterprise standards.”

**Reframe:** Federation is about where people sit and how local decisions are made. Enterprise can still own principles, cross-domain decision rights, and platform partnership.

## Misconception 4 — “Principles should name our standard technologies.”

**Reframe:** That turns principles into a shopping list that ages poorly. Put product choices in standards and ADRs; keep principles enduring.

## Misconception 5 — “If I have the Lead EA title, people will comply.”

**Reframe:** At NorthStar, BU power and delivery pressure dominate. Influence sources—credibility, sponsorship, process usefulness—matter more than title.

## Misconception 6 — “RACI means everyone is Consulted.”

**Reframe:** Over-consultation is delay. Consult those who change the decision; Inform others asynchronously.

## Misconception 7 — “Security can wait until Module 07.”

**Reframe:** Module 01 does not require threat models, but omitting Security from RACI/principles repeats NorthStar’s late-engagement failure. Proportionate inclusion is required.

## Misconception 8 — “Hybrid is always the best practice.”

**Reframe:** Hybrid fits NorthStar’s Year-1 constraints (acquired BUs, existing federated architects, transformation pressure). Another enterprise might centralize successfully. Force students to state *fit factors*.

## Misconception 9 — “Architecture mission should sound transformational.”

**Reframe:** Executives fund clarity. Prefer plain language: outcomes, decision scope, non-ownership. Jargon reduces sponsorship.

## Misconception 10 — “Exceptions prove principles failed.”

**Reframe:** Exceptions are how principles survive contact with reality—if time-boxed, owned, and visible. Invisible exceptions are the failure.

## Misconception 11 — “The operating model is the org chart.”

**Reframe:** Org chart is one input. Decision rights, engagement, cadence, and funding complete the model.

## Misconception 12 — “Documenting decisions slows us down.”

**Reframe:** Rework from undocumented conflicting decisions is slower. ADRs/memos are proportionate to decision class—not novels for every story.
