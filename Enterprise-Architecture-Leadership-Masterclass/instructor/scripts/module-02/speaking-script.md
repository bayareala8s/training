# Speaking Script — Module 02: Business Architecture and Capability Mapping

**Total runtime target:** 120 minutes  
**Tone:** Executive, practical, trade-off aware  
**Case study:** NorthStar Financial Services (fictional)

Instructors may paraphrase; timing cues are targets.

---

## [0:00–0:05] Welcome and framing

> Welcome back to the Enterprise Architecture Leadership Masterclass.  
> Today is Module 02: Business Architecture and Capability Mapping.  
> Remember: you are the newly appointed Lead Enterprise Architect at NorthStar Financial Services—our fictional enterprise case study.  
> By the end of this session you will turn strategy themes into a capability map, value-stream overlay, and investment heatmap that executives can argue with productively.

---

## [0:05–0:15] Business scenario

> NorthStar’s ExCo wants operating cost down twenty percent, faster onboarding, faster product launches, and better risk visibility—while acquired companies still run overlapping platforms. The CIO asks you for an architecture priority list. If your first move is a cloud migration spreadsheet, you skipped the hard part.

**Ask the room:**

> What question should you ask before naming a single system?

---

## [0:15–0:35] Architecture concept

> Strategy themes are not initiatives, and initiatives are not architecture. We translate themes into outcomes and KPIs, then into capabilities—what the business must be good at—then into investment themes and constraints.

**Whiteboard / slide:** Strategy → Outcomes → Capabilities

> A capability is org- and system-independent. “Payments mainframe” is not a capability. “Payment processing” is. We will classify Core, Supporting, and Commodity so we know where to differentiate versus standardize.

**Check for understanding:**

> Give me one NorthStar example that is commodity—and one that is truly core. Defend the line.

---

## [0:35–0:50] Instructor demonstration

> Watch how I would approach this as NorthStar’s Lead EA. I will make my trade-offs explicit—including what I am *not* choosing.

**Demo steps (narrate while doing):**

1. Draft an L1 set across customer, payments, partner, risk, platforms, corporate.
2. Expand Payment Processing or Customer Onboarding to L2; assign owners.
3. Sketch onboarding stages; mark identity and data frictions; state one investment theme and what we delay.

> Notice the artifact shape. Your lab should produce something an executive or ARB could consume.

---

## [0:50–1:30] Guided lab

> You now have about 40 minutes. Work in pairs if you want, but submissions are individual.  
> Lab: Build NorthStar’s Business Capability Map.  
> Deliverables: L1–L2 map with types and owners; onboarding value-stream overlay; heatmap with evidence.  
> I will circulate. At the halfway mark I will call a 2-minute progress check.

**[1:10] Mid-lab check**

> What decision is hardest right now—and what evidence would make it easier?

---

## [1:30–1:45] Architecture review

> Let’s review 1–2 volunteer artifacts. We will practice ARB-style questions: alignment, risk, alternatives, and feasibility—not nitpicks on formatting.

**Prompt:**

> Walk us from one KPI to one capability gap to one investment theme—and name the stakeholder who will resist.

---

## [1:45–1:55] Assignment briefing

> Deepen today’s lab into an executive capability investment brief. Include trade-offs and assumptions.  
> Rubric emphasis this week: business alignment and trade-off analysis.  
> Capstone link: your capability map and heatmap become durable inputs to the transformation narrative.

---

## [1:55–2:00] Close

> Key takeaways:  
> 1. Capabilities outlive org charts and systems.  
> 2. Value streams expose friction worth funding.  
> 3. Heatmaps are decision aids—evidence required.  
> Next module: Current-State Architecture Assessment—TIME and portfolio risk. Office hours: bring ownership debates.
