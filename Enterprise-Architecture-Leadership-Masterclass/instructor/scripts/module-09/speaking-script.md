# Speaking Script — Module 09: Architecture Governance and Executive Communication

**Total runtime target:** 120 minutes  
**Tone:** Executive, practical, trade-off aware  
**Case study:** NorthStar Financial Services (fictional)

Instructors may paraphrase; timing cues are targets.

---

## [0:00–0:05] Welcome and framing

> Welcome back. Today is Module 09: Architecture Governance and Executive Communication.  
> You are still NorthStar’s Lead Enterprise Architect—our fictional enterprise case study.  
> By the end of this session you will run an Architecture Review Board and leave with a CIO-ready disposition package.

---

## [0:05–0:15] Business scenario

> Retail Payments has funding, contractors, and a date. They want a second cloud, a proprietary database, a custom integration framework, and standing production admin for contractors.  
> The CIO asks whether governance is blocking innovation—or preventing a permanent tax.

**Ask the room:**

> If the ARB met in five minutes with no analysis, who would approve all four requests? Show of hands. Hold that thought.

---

## [0:15–0:35] Architecture concept

> Governance is a product: principles, standards, golden paths, guardrails, exceptions, and selective human forums.  
> If teams only meet governance as a late “no,” they will route around it.

**Whiteboard / slide:** Governance stack

> NorthStar needs risk-tiered review. Today’s proposal is Tier 2 four times over.

**Check for understanding:**

> What is the difference between a standard and a guardrail?

---

## [0:35–0:50] Instructor demonstration

> Watch a compressed ARB on contractor production access only. I will make trade-offs explicit—including what I am not choosing.

**Demo steps (narrate while doing):**

1. Frame the decision and non-goals.  
2. Challenge standing admin vs. PAM/JIT.  
3. Dispose: Reject standing access; Approve time-bound JIT with session recording; name owners.

> Notice the artifact shape: disposition sentence, conditions, ADR title, memo bullet.

---

## [0:50–1:30] Guided lab

> You now have about 40 minutes for the full four-request ARB.  
> Lab: Conduct an Architecture Review Board.  
> Deliverables: checklist, scorecard, memo, ADRs.  
> I will circulate. At the halfway mark I will call a 2-minute progress check.

**[1:10] Mid-lab check**

> What decision is hardest right now—and what evidence would make it easier?

---

## [1:30–1:45] Architecture review

> Let’s review 1–2 volunteer dispositions. We will practice ARB-style questions: alignment, risk, alternatives, and feasibility—not nitpicks on formatting.

**Prompt:**

> Read your recommendation in one sentence. Then name the precedent risk.

---

## [1:45–1:55] Assignment briefing

> Polish the package for BayLearn submission. Rubric emphasis this week: trade-off analysis, security and resilience, communication quality.  
> Capstone link: governance model, executive memo, and ADRs toward the five-ADR set.

---

## [1:55–2:00] Close

> Key takeaways:  
> 1. Split independent decisions.  
> 2. Conditions need owners and tests.  
> 3. Executives need an ask, not a diagram pack.  
> Next module: Capstone and Architecture Leadership. Office hours: bring draft memo if stuck.
