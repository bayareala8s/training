# Instructor Standards

## Purpose

Ensure consistent, executive-quality live delivery across BayLearn cohorts and corporate workshops.

---

## Required instructor package (per module)

```text
module-XX/
├── instructor-guide.md
├── speaking-script.md
├── whiteboard-plan.md
├── discussion-questions.md
├── common-misconceptions.md
├── lab-facilitation-guide.md
├── reference-solution.md
├── grading-guide.md
└── slide-notes.md
```

Locations:

- Canonical copies under `instructor/guides/module-XX/` (and related folders)
- Module folders may link to instructor assets; **never** place reference solutions in student packages

---

## Instructor guide requirements

Each guide must include:

1. Module purpose  
2. Learning objectives  
3. Prerequisites  
4. Estimated timing  
5. Opening business scenario  
6. Lesson flow  
7. Questions to ask  
8. Whiteboard sequence  
9. Demonstration steps  
10. Break points  
11. Lab instructions (facilitation view)  
12. Common student issues  
13. Debrief questions  
14. Assignment briefing  
15. Suggested homework  

---

## Speaking script standards

- Word-for-word usable, but instructors may paraphrase
- Mark timing cues (`[0:00]`, `[0:15]`, etc.)
- Mark whiteboard / slide transitions
- Include prompts for student discussion
- Use NorthStar language consistently
- Avoid claiming affiliation with real employers

---

## Facilitation principles

| Principle | Practice |
| --------- | -------- |
| Trade-offs over dogma | Never present one pattern as universally correct |
| Business first | Open with NorthStar outcomes, not technology |
| Make thinking visible | Whiteboard decisions and rejected alternatives |
| Separate solution keys | Students struggle productively; solutions stay instructor-only |
| Timebox labs | Protect debrief; incomplete labs can finish as homework |
| Psychological safety | Challenge ideas, not people—especially in ARB role-play |

---

## Grading standards

- Use the standard architecture rubric unless a module rubric explicitly overrides criterion weights
- Provide actionable feedback: what to improve for the capstone portfolio
- Flag security, cost, and resilience gaps even on otherwise strong submissions
- Score participation in ARB labs on preparation and constructive challenge

---

## AWS lab facilitation (Modules 5–8)

- Confirm student accounts and budget alarms before class
- Demonstrate cleanup at the end of every AWS session
- State estimated cost verbally and in chat
- Have a demo account ready if student deploy fails
- Never require NAT Gateway, EKS, OpenSearch, or always-on EC2 for core credit

---

## Slide and branding standards

- 15–25 slides per module
- 16:9
- Dark navy, white, restrained gold accents
- Minimal text; speaker notes carry detail
- No cartoonish imagery
- Executive-quality diagrams

---

## Office hours

- Prioritize blockers that stop artifact completion
- Capstone coaching from Week 7 onward
- Do not re-teach the full live session; point to recordings/scripts
