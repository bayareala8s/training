# Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students must be able to:

## Enterprise architecture leadership

1. Explain the role of an enterprise architect and how it differs from solution, cloud, platform, and engineering leadership roles.
2. Influence architecture outcomes without relying solely on hierarchical authority.
3. Lead architecture review conversations and defend trade-offs under executive scrutiny.

## Business and capability architecture

4. Translate business strategy into architecture priorities, constraints, and investment themes.
5. Build business capability maps (Level 1–3) with ownership, maturity, and heatmaps.
6. Connect value streams, stakeholders, KPIs, and technology investments with outcome traceability.

## Current-state assessment

7. Assess current-state architecture without producing an unmanageable inventory.
8. Apply application portfolio techniques including the TIME model (Tolerate, Invest, Migrate, Eliminate).
9. Identify technical debt, concentration risk, operational burden, and security/resilience gaps.

## Target state and roadmaps

10. Design target-state architectures aligned to principles, constraints, and business outcomes.
11. Create transition architectures and migration waves that manage coexistence and risk.
12. Build sequenced roadmaps that show funding logic, value realization, and milestones.

## Cloud and platforms

13. Define cloud and platform strategies including landing zones, shared services, and guardrails.
14. Distinguish build-versus-buy decisions for platform capabilities and document them as ADRs.
15. Apply FinOps practices: tagging, budgets, cost allocation, and service lifecycle controls.

## Integration, application, and data

16. Select integration and data patterns based on latency, coupling, volume, reliability, security, cost, and operational complexity.
17. Design application architectures with clear domains, ownership, and shared-platform boundaries.
18. Articulate data product, master data, and metadata ownership models.

## Security, risk, compliance, and resilience

19. Design security, resilience, and compliance controls as first-class architecture concerns.
20. Perform lightweight threat modeling and map controls to regulatory obligations and evidence.
21. Define RTO/RPO targets and validate recovery approaches.

## AI strategy

22. Develop an enterprise AI strategy tied to measurable business outcomes and data readiness.
23. Design governed AI architectures including structured outputs, validation, human-in-the-loop, audit, and cost tracking.
24. Evaluate AI use cases for value, feasibility, risk, and operating-model fit.

## Governance and communication

25. Establish architecture governance using standards, automated guardrails, exceptions, and review boards.
26. Write Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) with context, alternatives, and consequences.
27. Present architecture recommendations to executives in business language.
28. Build a professional architecture portfolio and personal leadership development plan.
