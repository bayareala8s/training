## Fiction notice

NorthStar Financial Services is a **fictional** instructional case study created for BayLearn. It is not affiliated with any real company, bank, or employer.

---

## Module objective

Establish yourself as NorthStar’s Lead Enterprise Architect by defining what the architecture function does, how it engages the enterprise, and how it influences outcomes without relying solely on hierarchical authority.

By the end of this module you will have a first-cut **architecture operating model**—mission, principles, RACI, decision rights, engagement model, and a risk register—that executives and business-unit architects can react to.

---

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

1. Distinguish enterprise architecture from solution, cloud, platform, and engineering leadership roles—and explain the value EA creates at NorthStar.
2. Design an architecture operating model (mission, structure options, decision rights, engagement) suited to a federated financial-services enterprise.
3. Draft 8–10 architecture principles with rationale, implications, exception paths, and measurable signals.
4. Assess personal and organizational architecture-leadership readiness, including influence without authority.

## NorthStar starting condition (this week)

Architects already exist inside business units (Retail Banking, Payments, Partner Channels, Wealth). Each BU optimizes locally. There is **no consistent enterprise governance**, no shared principles, and no clear decision rights for cross-domain standards. The CIO has asked you—newly appointed Lead EA—to propose how architecture should work before the next Executive Committee technology review.
