# Learning Objectives — Module 01: The Enterprise Architect’s Role

**Course outcomes primarily addressed:** 1, 2, 3 (enterprise architecture leadership)  
**Secondary foreshadowing:** 25–27 (governance and communication)

---

## Module-level objectives

By the end of Module 01, students will be able to:

| ID | Objective | Bloom | Assessment |
| -- | --------- | ----- | ---------- |
| M01-LO1 | Explain what enterprise architecture is at NorthStar—and how it differs from solution, cloud, platform, and engineering leadership—using business outcomes, not job titles alone. | Understand / Analyze | Quiz Q1–Q2, Q6; Lesson 1.1 discussion; Assignment narrative |
| M01-LO2 | Design a federated-compatible architecture operating model including mission, structural option, decision rights, RACI, and engagement model. | Apply / Create | Lab deliverables; Assignment; Quiz S1–S2 |
| M01-LO3 | Write 8–10 architecture principles with statement, rationale, implications, exception path, and success signals tied to NorthStar strategy. | Apply / Create | Lab principles; Quiz Q3–Q4, Q7; Assignment |
| M01-LO4 | Assess architecture leadership readiness (self and organization) and prescribe influence tactics that do not depend solely on hierarchical authority. | Evaluate / Apply | Lesson 1.4; Quiz Q5, Q8–Q10, S3, D1–D2; Workbook |

---

## Lesson-level outcomes

### Lesson 1.1 — What EA Really Is

- Contrast EA vs. SA / platform / cloud / engineering leadership using decision scope and time horizon.
- Name the failure modes when EA is treated as “senior designer of everything.”

### Lesson 1.2 — Architecture Operating Model

- Compare centralized, federated, and hybrid operating models with explicit trade-offs for NorthStar’s acquired-BU structure.
- Define engagement patterns (consult → collaborate → govern) and when each fits.

### Lesson 1.3 — Architecture Principles

- Convert strategy themes into principles that constrain decisions without becoming a technology shopping list.
- Design an exception process that protects risk while preserving delivery speed.

### Lesson 1.4 — Architecture Leadership Assessment

- Score influence sources (expertise, network, process design, executive sponsorship, delivery credibility).
- Identify the first 90-day leadership moves for a newly appointed Lead EA with weak formal authority.

---

## Success criteria (observable)

A student who meets the objectives can produce artifacts that:

1. An executive sponsor can skim in under 10 minutes and understand “what architecture will do for us.”
2. A BU architect can see where they retain autonomy and where enterprise standards apply.
3. A CISO or Risk partner can see that security/resilience is designed into principles—not bolted on later.
4. The student can defend *why they rejected* a fully centralized ARB-heavy model for NorthStar’s Year-1 reality.
