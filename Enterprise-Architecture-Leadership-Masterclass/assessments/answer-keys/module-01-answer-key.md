## Scenario guidance

### S1 — Expected elements

- Year-1 recommendation typically **option 2 (federated + EA office + narrow ARB)** given existing BU architects and acquisition politics—unless student makes a strong, explicit case for centralization with executive willingness.  
- Must state **benefit** (coalition, speed, lower reorg cost) and **accepted risk** (slower consistency).  
- Mitigation examples: decision classes, co-authored principles, CIO digest, measured ARB triggers.  
- Penalize “hybrid is best practice” without NorthStar fit factors.

### S2 — Expected elements

- Failure modes: too many items, vendor lock-in as “principle,” no exceptions → wallpaper or underground work.  
- Reshape: map to strategy themes; 8–10 principles with anatomy; move vendors to standards/ADRs; add exception path and signals.  
- Mention coexistence / financial-services constraints.

### S3 — Expected elements

- Refuse heavy gate-for-everything while paths and sponsorship are weak.  
- Propose smallest viable increment: principles + decision classes + narrow material triggers + one credibility win + risk visibility.  
- Still take risk seriously: Security consult on material classes; exception register; escalate true lock-in/identity/AI-high-risk items.  
- Tie to “credibility before control.”

---

## Discussion facilitation notes

### D1

Look for: escalation thresholds tied to decision class/material risk—not ego; sponsorship use as cover for contested enterprise decisions; risk that premature escalation burns peer trust. No single correct script.

### D2

Look for: retained local autonomy for in-guardrail solution choices; enterprise accountability for principles, shared platforms, material exceptions, identity/data cross-BU changes. Strong answers name friction with BU presidents as expected, not failure.
