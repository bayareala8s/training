## Slide 2 — Business scenario

**On slide:**  
NorthStar Financial Services *(fictional)*  
Architects in every BU · No consistent governance  
“Three overlapping identity projects funded”  
CIO ask: operating model before ExCo tech review

**Notes:** Read fiction notice aloud. Paint acquired-estate fragmentation, late security, duplicate platforms. Cold-call: if architecture disappeared, which outcome fails first? Keep discussion to ~3 minutes.

---

## Slide 3 — Learning objectives

**On slide:**  
1. Distinguish EA from related roles  
2. Design an operating model with decision rights  
3. Write 8–10 principles with exceptions  
4. Influence without relying on title alone

**Notes:** Map each objective to a lab deliverable (mission/roles → model/RACI → principles → leadership narrative).

---

## Slide 4 — What EA really is

**On slide:**  
Enterprise architecture = leadership system  
Strategy → technology direction across domains  
Not a drawing service · Not owner of every backlog

**Notes:** Define EA in outcome language. Kill the “senior designer of everything” myth early. Emphasize decision quality, reuse, risk visibility.

---

## Slide 5 — Role boundaries

**On slide:**  
Table: EA | Domain | SA | Platform/Cloud | Eng Manager  
Columns: primary question · horizon · ownership signal

**Notes:** Walk one row at a time. Ask where a second API gateway decision sits. Overlapping titles OK; overlapping accountabilities without rights = theater.

Vertical stack visual: Outcomes → Capabilities → Themes → Principles/Rights → Platforms/Guardrails → Solutions  
Gold accent on Principles/Rights

**Notes:** Draw on whiteboard in parallel if possible. Teach “climb the stack” when vendors appear. This board stays visible all session.

## Slide 7 — Value executives fund

**On slide:**  
Cost · Speed · Risk visibility · Reuse  
Leading vs lagging indicators (2 examples max)

**Notes:** If EA cannot name indicators, it will be judged on slide aesthetics. Avoid wiki-hit vanity metrics.

## Slide 8 — Operating model elements

**On slide:**  
Mission · Structure · Decision rights · RACI  
Engagement · Cadence · Capacity  
Org chart ≠ operating model

**Notes:** Students often deliver only an org chart—pre-empt that failure mode.

---

## Slide 9 — Structural options

**On slide:**  
Central | Federated | Hybrid  
2 pros / 2 cons each  
★ Hybrid fits NorthStar Year 1 — not universal dogma

**Notes:** Force trade-offs. Star hybrid as *fit* because BU architects already exist. Invite dissenters to defend alternatives with accepted risks.

---

## Slide 10 — Engagement modes

**On slide:**  
Consult → Collaborate → Govern  
Guardrails (automate) vs Gates (human)  
Gold callout: Bias to guardrails in Year 1

**Notes:** Misuse creates bottleneck or irrelevance. Define guardrail vs gate using course glossary.

## Slide 13 — Principle anatomy

**On slide:**  
Name · Statement · Rationale · Implications · Exceptions · Signals  
❌ “Be cloud-native”  
✅ “Prefer platform golden paths”

**Notes:** Live-kill shopping-list principles. Aim 8–10. Strategy themes → principle themes mapping in one breath.

---

## Slide 14 — Exception path

**On slide:**  
Request → Risk assessment → Decision → Expiry/review  
Time-boxed exceptions beat permanent special cases

**Notes:** Without exceptions, principles become lies; uncontrolled exceptions become wallpaper. Keep process usable under delivery pressure.

---

## Slide 15 — Influence without authority

**On slide:**  
Sources: Expertise · Network · Process · Sponsorship · Credibility · Evidence  
Sequence: Listen → Frame → Principles → One win → Risk reporting → Expand rights

**Notes:** Title is insufficient at NorthStar. Credibility before control. Tease 90-day plan for assignment.
